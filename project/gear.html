Gear Guide

You do not need expensive equipment to start hiking. You do need the right layers, enough water and a plan. Here is what our members carry on a day hike in cold weather.

Dress in layers

Base layer

A thin top that wicks sweat away from your skin. Wool or synthetic fabric works well. Avoid cotton, which stays wet and cold.

Middle layer

A fleece or light down jacket that keeps your body heat in. Take it off when you warm up on the climb.

Outer layer

A waterproof, windproof shell. Wind and rain on exposed ridges can make a mild day feel very cold.

Head and hands

A warm hat and light gloves weigh almost nothing and make a big difference at the summit.

Footwear

Wear shoes with a grippy sole that you have already worn in. Trail shoes are enough for easy and moderate hikes. Boots with ankle support are better for steep, rocky routes.

Always carry

  • At least two liters of water
  • Snacks such as nuts, fruit or energy bars
  • A fully charged phone and a power bank
  • Sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat
  • A small first aid kit
  • A headlamp if you might finish near dusk

Staying safe

  • Check the weather forecast before you leave, and turn back if clouds roll in.
  • Tell someone your route and when you expect to return.
  • Stay on marked paths and keep your group together.
  • In an emergency, call 112 from a mobile phone.
